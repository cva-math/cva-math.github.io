What you just saw

Each limit on its own splits the plane in two — everything on one side is allowed, everything on the other isn’t. What you were hunting for is the patch where all of them agree at once. And notice what it is: not one right answer with wrong ones around it, but a whole area of weeks, every one of them fine.

The names for this, when you’re ready for them: an inequality in two variables shades a half-plane, and its edge is the boundary line — drawn solid when the edge itself counts (≤, ≥) and dashed when it doesn’t (<, >). Several of them at once make a system of inequalities, and the overlap is the feasible region. Written down, this week was x ≤ 12, x + y ≤ 40, 24x + 17y ≥ 600. Feasible just means: these are the ones you could actually do.

What you just saw

At $764 there is exactly one week left: all twelve tutoring hours and twenty‑eight at the café. One dollar past it there is nothing — not a hard week, not a wrong answer, nothing. An empty region is a real result, and it says something precise: not with these constraints, it can’t. Which makes the next question the only one left. Which constraint moves?

The names: a system whose shadings never overlap has no solution, and its region is empty. That is an answer, not a failure to find one — the same way 0 = 5 at the end of solving a system tells you the two lines were parallel all along. And a constraint is not a law of nature. Some of these you can move — what the job pays, what you need to clear. Some of them you can’t: the week is still forty hours long.

Then The Anaheim Card

One more region, and the page won’t graph this one for you.

Anaheim · three constraints
  1. r ≤ 1.3 · w · h the 30% rule, written as an inequality
  2. h ≤ 40 a week only has so many working hours
  3. r ≥ 1,618 half a median two-bedroom — nothing here is cheaper
w is what you earn an hour, h the hours you work in a week, r the rent you could carry. Anaheim’s own minimum wage law, Measure L, puts w at $21.13.

You have done this shape twice now — a ceiling, a floor, and a line that moves when a wage does. Graph it. What does the region look like — and if it turns out there is nothing in it, which line would you move?

When the answer isn’t a number it’s a region — and an empty one is still an answer. It names the thing that has to change.
