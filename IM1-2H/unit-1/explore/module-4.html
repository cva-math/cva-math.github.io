What you just saw

Last week every combination swung around the crossing. These never swing, because there is no crossing to swing around — every line you built came out parallel to the other two, just somewhere else. And the moment you tried to cancel a letter, you cancelled both, and what was left said 0 = 5. That isn’t a slip in your algebra. It is the algebra telling you, in the only way it can, that no point anywhere satisfies both equations. There is nowhere to stand.

The names for this, when you’re ready for them: the system has no solution, and the lines are parallel — the same slope, different intercepts. A leftover like 0 = 5 is a contradiction: a statement no value can make true. And here is how to spot it in the algebra alone. Last week the x-number and the y-number reached zero at different k — −3 and 2 — and each one handed you a number. Here they reach zero at the same k, together. That coincidence is what parallel looks like when you can’t see the graph.

What you just saw

Every equation you built drew the same line — different numbers, the same points — because each one was just the first equation stretched. Nothing on the chart could move. And when both letters cancelled, the number cancelled with them, and what was left said 0 = 0: true for every x and every y. A statement that is always true rules nothing out. So you are left with the line itself, and every point on it is an answer.

The names: the system has infinitely many solutions — every point on the line — because the two equations are equivalent: one is a multiple of the other. A leftover like 0 = 0 is an identity, true for every value. So elimination always finishes in one of three ways: x = 4, and there is one solution; 0 = 5, and there is none; 0 = 0, and there are infinitely many. One point, nowhere, or everywhere on the line.

Then The Card

One question the page won’t answer for you.

Every system of two straight lines

One. · None. · Infinitely many.

Every system you have met has landed in one of those three. Never exactly two answers. Never seven.

Why not? Try to draw two straight lines that cross in exactly two places. If you can’t, say what stops you — and what it has to do with 0 = 0.

Elimination never breaks. One answer, and it hands you a number. None, and it hands you a lie. Infinitely many, and it hands you the truth.
