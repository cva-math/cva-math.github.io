The Crossover

Two people, same graduating class. One goes through college; one goes straight to work. This unit runs on one sentence: whether college is worth it depends on which trade-off you can live with. Nobody settles that by being told. This page is built so you can watch it — four instruments, two lines.

What you just saw

A line is a life. Where it starts is what school cost you and how long it took. How steep it climbs is what a year of work adds. Every argument in this unit is built out of those two numbers.

The name for this, when you’re ready for it: y = 60000(t − 4) − 22500  — the 4 is the years in school; the 22,500 is the hole she climbs out of.

What you just saw

The head start is real: less debt, three more years of paychecks. So is the steeper climb. Who’s ahead now and who ends up ahead are two different questions, and most arguments about college quietly answer only one of them.

Two lives, one set of axes: y = 60000(t − 4) − 22500 and y = 38000(t − 1) − 3000  — two equations sharing one t. That is a system.

What you just saw

One point belongs to both lives at once. That is what solving a system means — not two answers, but the single moment where two different stories agree. Before it, the head start wins. After it, the climb does.

Set them equal and solve: 60000(t − 4) − 22500 = 38000(t − 1) − 3000  — one unknown, one line of algebra, t ≈ 10.1.

Act IV Break It

Now make the crossover disappear. There is more than one way — and they mean different things.

These are the real numbers, and they are yours to move. Change a wage, a year of school, a debt — or put your own two careers in and use wages from the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook. The verdict underneath rewrites itself as you drag.

What you just saw

One, none, or infinitely many is not a classification exercise. It is three different answers to the question this unit asks: it pays off in year X — it never pays off in dollars at all — or the money is a wash and something else has to decide.

Same climb, different start → no solution.  Same climb, same start → infinitely many.  The shape of the answer is itself an answer.

Coda The Part the Graph Can’t See

Every number on this page is true. None of it is the whole story.

  • AI is changing entry-level pay. A lot of work that used to need a degree is getting compressed. Wage data from 2023 may overstate what someone starting today actually earns over the next ten years.
  • Real careers don’t run in straight lines. Both of these assume the same raise-free wage for thirty years. Nobody has that — there are promotions, layoffs, career changes, years off.
  • Money is one variable. Neither line says anything about what kind of life each path makes possible, and that is usually the part people actually decide on.
Which is the assignment

At the end of this unit you make the case — your two paths, your crossover, your call — to someone who knows you. The math is the evidence. The verdict is yours.
