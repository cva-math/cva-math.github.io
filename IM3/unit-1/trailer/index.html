The Shape of Money

This unit runs on one sentence: time is the most valuable thing you have with money. Nobody believes that sentence by being told it. This page is built so you can see it — five instruments, one curve.

The dollars added get bigger every single year — but the rule never changes. Money doesn’t grow by adding. It grows by multiplying, and multiplication compounds.

The name for this, when you’re ready for it: A = 1000(1.07)t  — the 1.07 is the machine; t is how long you let it run.

Act III The Flip

New question. Not “what will I have in 30 years?” — “when will I hit $8,000?”

$1,000 at 7% again. Slide the target to a dollar amount you care about, and watch the curve answer with a time. You’re reading the curve backwards. Then press the button.

Target
$8,000
The curve answers
30.7 years
What you just saw

That’s the whole secret of this unit. You didn’t learn a new function — you turned the old one sideways. The backwards reading has a name: the logarithm. Exponents answer how much. Logs answer how long.

The name for the flip: t = log(8) / log(1.07) ≈ 30.7 — log is the button that reads the curve backwards. Your calculator has it. Now you know what it’s pressing.

Act IV The Investor’s Ruler

Same money. New ruler. Stop counting dollars — start comparing them.

The normal axis counts: $20k is one step above $10k, and $30k one step above that. The ratio ruler compares: one step means “×2,” wherever you stand. Switch and watch the curve.

On the ratio ruler, steady multiplication is a straight line — and every doubling takes the same 10.2 years. This is the axis every serious market chart is drawn on. You can read those charts now.

The ratio ruler’s real name: a logarithmic scale — the log from Act III, used as a lens instead of a button.

Coda The Speed Limit

One dollar. 100% interest. One year. Compound it more and more often — where does it stop?

Once a year turns $1 into $2. Twice a year does better. Every day, better still. It looks like it should run to infinity. It doesn’t.

Compounded
once a year
$1 becomes
$2.00000
What you just saw

Compounding has a natural ceiling. Push the frequency to infinity and $1 lands on 2.71828… — a number the universe picked, not a person. Its name is e, and it’s waiting for you in Module 3.

The name for the ceiling: e ≈ 2.71828 — the base of every continuously-growing thing: money, populations, spread.

You now own the two directions of one curve: how much, and how long. The rest of the unit is learning to point them at your own life.
