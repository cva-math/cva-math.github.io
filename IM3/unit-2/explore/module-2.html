Touch and Go

Write a polynomial as factors, and every one of them leaves a mark where the graph meets the x-axis. Not every mark looks the same. Drag the marks around and see for yourself.

Drag a dot — or click one and use the arrow keys.

What you just saw

Every factor pins the graph to the x-axis at one spot — and it isn’t the spot the sign suggests. (x − 4) crosses at 4, and (x + 3) crosses at −3. A product is zero whenever any one of its pieces is zero, and x − 4 is zero exactly when x is 4. Park a dot at 0 and its factor is just x.

The name for this, when you’re ready for it: the places a graph meets the x-axis are the zeros of the polynomial. A factor (x − a) makes a zero at x = a.

What you just saw

Power 1 goes straight through the axis. Power 2 comes down, touches it, and turns back — it never gets to the other side. Power 3 does get through, but it flattens out on the way, almost stopping. Odd powers cross. Even powers turn back. And every extra power on a factor adds one to the degree.

The name for this, when you’re ready for it: the power on a factor is its multiplicity. A zero with odd multiplicity crosses the axis; a zero with even multiplicity touches it and turns back.

Every factor leaves a mark on the axis. Now you know how to read them.
