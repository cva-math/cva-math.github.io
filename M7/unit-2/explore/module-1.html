Eight Percent of Fifty

Some percentages come straight off the line and some take real work. Eight percent of fifty takes real work. The strange part is that you never have to do it.

Color it in. Swap the numbers. Watch what stays.
Act I The Swap

Color in 8% of 50. Then swap the two numbers and color in 50% of 8.

The line and the grid are the same whole, 50. Every tick is another 10%, and every square is 1%. What you just saw

Everything changed. The whole went from 50 to 8, the ticks went from fives to eight-tenths, and each square went from worth 0.5 to worth 0.08. You colored 8 squares the first time and 50 the second. The answer stayed at 4. Eight percent of fifty and fifty percent of eight are the same amount — so when one of them is hard, do the other one.

The name for this, when you’re ready for it: 8% means eight hundredths, so 8% of 50 is 8 × 50 ÷ 100 and 50% of 8 is 50 × 8 ÷ 100 — 8 squares of 0.5, or 50 squares of 0.08. Same three numbers, multiplied in a different order. Multiplying doesn’t care about order — that’s the commutative property, hiding inside a percent.

What you just saw

Every pair came out the same, whichever number you called the percent. So you can always turn a hard one around: 4% of 75 is 75% of 4, which is 3. And 16% of 25 is 25% of 16, which is 4. Neither one needs a calculator once it’s the right way around.

Written once, for any two numbers: a% of b = b% of a, because both are a × b ÷ 100 — a squares worth b ÷ 100 each, or b squares worth a ÷ 100 each. Every percent-of on this unit’s Pages had a twin like this. You only ever needed to find the easier one.

Then The Card

One small word the page won’t test for you.

One word changed

Ten percent more than forty.

10% of 40 is 40% of 10 — you’ve seen why. Now change of to more than. Ten percent more than forty, and forty percent more than ten. Does the swap still work? If it doesn’t, what broke it?

Work both out. Then say what more than is doing that of wasn’t.
