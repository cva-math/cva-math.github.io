Thirty-Seven and a Half

For two weeks you found percents on a double number line. This week the line goes away and a letter takes its place. Here is why it has to: some percents would take four hundred ticks to draw.

What you just saw

37% took a hundred pieces. 37.5% — the one that looks harder — took eight, because 37.5% is exactly three eighths. And 7.25% took four hundred: the ticks ran together into a smear. The picture charges you for how finely a percent is cut, not for how big it is.

The name for this, when you’re ready for it: write the percent as a fraction in lowest terms, and the pieces it needs is the denominator. 37.5% is 3/8, so eight. 37% is 37/100, so a hundred. And 7.25% is 29/400 — four hundred pieces for one answer.

What you just saw

Only a handful of squares are light — 50%, the quarters, the fifths, the tenths. Forty of the hundred need all hundred pieces. But the letter treats every square the same: turn the percent into a decimal and multiply once. That’s why the picture goes away. It’s lovely for the friendly percents, and the letter works for all of them.

Written once, for any whole: y = 0.37x. The decimal in front is the percent, and x is the whole — 80 here, and anything at all tomorrow.
